What changes in the player preview?

This replaces the browser-local menu with four actions priced at 20, 60, 120 and 240 tokens, using the captured premium and pacing. It creates a new wheel version and preserves the wallet, old spins, pending prizes and original refund amounts. The operator can still edit action prices and premium.

The player preview uses whole tokens and integer draw precision, so its price and actual win frequency differ slightly from the continuous simulation. The preview does not run the simulated audience, enforce its 24-action quota, or automatically switch offers. Use the replay to see that behavior.

What the experiment does—and doesn’t—show

The selected policy uses an 800% premium / 10% win offer at lower supply pressure and a 400% premium / 5% win offer at higher pressure. Those high premiums emerged from synthetic assumptions. They are not live pricing recommendations.

It improves mean contribution versus direct-only sales in all seven tested worlds. Adaptation still loses to a good fixed offer for the curious audience. Long-term retention, perceived fairness and reputation are not modeled, and 5% is the lower win-frequency boundary of this search.

The controller observes recent aggregate direct demand, queue work and remaining action slots. It does not access private willingness, viewer budgets or individual loss histories.
